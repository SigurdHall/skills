# Pitch And Proposal Narrative

Use this for proposals, mandate requests, initiatives, role arguments, funding,
project approval, buy-in, product pitches, or stakeholder persuasion.

## Sources And Influences

- Common pitch sequence: problem -> insight -> solution -> proof -> plan -> ask.
- YC-style pitching: quickly establish what matters, why now, evidence, and ask.
- Minto-style communication: answer first, then support.

## Pitch Workflow

1. Define the ask: approval, mandate, money, time, people, access, decision,
   experiment, or follow-up.
2. Name the audience's problem in their language.
3. Add the insight: what most people are missing or underestimating.
4. Present the solution as a focused response to the problem, not as a list of
   features.
5. Show proof: traction, evidence, prototype, local work, external example,
   benchmark, or risk reduction.
6. Show execution: first steps, owner, timeline, cost, constraints.
7. End with an explicit ask and decision path.

## Proposal Spine

`current pain -> why now -> proposed move -> proof -> trade-offs -> first step -> ask`

## Role Or Mandate Narrative

When the proposal involves the user's own role, keep the order strict:

1. Organizational need.
2. Gap between existing functions.
3. Work that must be done.
4. Why the user is a credible fit.
5. Small mandate or pilot.
6. How to avoid person dependency.

Do not let the narrative sound like the organization should adapt to the
person. The person should be positioned as a practical response to a real need.

## Objection Handling

Prepare answers for:

- Why now?
- Why this scope?
- Why this person/team?
- Why not keep doing the current process?
- What is the minimum viable version?
- What could fail?
- How will we know if this worked?
- What does it cost in attention, time, money, risk, or dependencies?
